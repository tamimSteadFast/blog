The Coway Airmega 200M is the unit we keep recommending for ordinary bedrooms,
and after six weeks living with one it's easy to see why. It does the two things
a bedroom purifier has to do — stay quiet and stay cheap to run — better than
anything else at its size.

## The short version

This is not the fastest air cleaner we've tested, and it's not meant to be. In a
room up to about 40 m² you will almost never need its top setting, which means
you get the benefit of a genuinely quiet purifier without paying for it in either
noise or filter cost. If your room is bigger than that, read our
[large-room pick](/blog/best-air-purifiers/) instead.

## Noise: the number that matters most

We ran the 200M on its highest setting from one metre away in a closed room and
measured 48 dB over ten minutes — the second-quietest top setting in our group,
behind only a unit that costs far more to run. More to the point, on the medium
setting that actually clears a bedroom overnight, it settles into a low, even
hum that disappears within a minute of lying down. The display stays lit but
dims in the dark, so it doesn't light up the room.

That restraint is the whole case for this purifier. A unit that cleans a little
faster but forces you to choose between clean air and sleep isn't a better
product; it's a worse one dressed up with a higher CADR figure.

## Running cost over three years

The other number we lead with is what it costs to keep going. Genuine
replacement filters for the 200M worked out to roughly $58 a year in our
tracking — the lowest of the group — and vacuuming the washable pre-filter once a
month noticeably extended the main filter's useful life. Over three years that
gap compounds: a purifier with cheaper upfront pricing but pricier filters can
easily cost more to own, which is exactly why we project filter cost out over
three years rather than quoting a single replacement price.

## Living with it

Day to day, the 200M is refreshingly boring, which is high praise for an
appliance that runs constantly. There's no app and no scheduling — you set a fan
speed, or let the auto mode respond to the built-in air-quality sensor, and
forget about it. For most people that's a feature, not a limitation. The controls
are physical buttons on top, lit but dimmable, and the filter-change indicator is
honest rather than aggressive.

The footprint is a tidy square that tucks beside a nightstand, and the intake is
on the front, so it doesn't need much clearance behind it.

## Where it falls short

No product earns a place here without a genuine drawback, and the 200M has a few.
It runs out of headroom in rooms larger than about 40 m² — push it into a big
open-plan space and you'll be stuck on the loud top setting to keep up, which
throws away its main advantage. There's no app, if scheduling or remote control
matters to you. And the pre-filter really does need vacuuming monthly; skip that
and both performance and filter life drop off faster than you'd expect.

## Who should buy it

Buy the Airmega 200M if you want a quiet, cheap-to-run purifier for a bedroom,
nursery, or small-to-mid room and you don't care about apps. Skip it if your room
is large, or if remote scheduling is a must-have. For most readers of this site —
first-time buyers who care most about noise and running cost — it's the one we'd
pick.

## Setup and first impressions

There's almost nothing to set up. You unbox the unit, peel the plastic wrap off
the filter (easy to forget, and the single most common reason a new purifier
seems weak), slot it back in, and plug it in. The 200M is running within a minute,
and the auto mode starts responding to the air-quality sensor straight away —
useful for a quick sanity check that it's working, because you'll see it spin up
when you spray something or light a candle nearby.

Build quality is solid without being flashy. The panels fit cleanly, the unit
doesn't rattle at any speed, and the whole thing is light enough to move between
rooms if you want to. The sensor sits behind a small grille on the side; keep it
clear of furniture so it reads the room rather than a wall.

## How it compares

Against the rest of the field, the 200M's case is consistency. The Winix 5500-2
is cheaper to run but louder and fussier; the Levoit Core 600S is faster but
pricier to feed and clearly audible in a big room; the Blueair 311i Max matches
it on quietness but costs a little more over three years. The Coway is the one
that doesn't force a compromise in a normal bedroom, which is why it's our overall
pick in the [full roundup](/blog/best-air-purifiers/). If your needs sit at an
extreme — a very large room, or the absolute lowest running cost — one of those
alternatives may suit you better.
